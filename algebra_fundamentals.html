Algebra Fundamentals

Master the building blocks of algebraic expressions and learn how to break down complex equations.

1. The Building Blocks of Algebra

What is Algebra?

Algebra is the branch of mathematics that uses letters (called variables) and symbols to represent unknown numbers and relationships between quantities. Instead of dealing only with concrete numbers, algebra allows us to work with general relationships that apply to many situations.

Variables

Variable: A symbol (usually a letter like $x$, $y$, or $z$) that represents an unknown or changeable number.
Example: In the expression $3x + 5$, the letter $x$ is a variable. It could represent any number: 1, 2, 100, or even a fraction.

Terms

Term: A single mathematical expression that consists of a number (coefficient) and/or variable(s) multiplied together. Terms are separated by addition ($+$) or subtraction ($−$) signs.
Example: In $5x + 3y − 7$, there are three terms: $5x$, $3y$, and $−7$.

Coefficients

Coefficient: The number that multiplies a variable. It tells you how many of that variable you have.
Example: In $7x$, the coefficient is $7$. In $4xy$, the coefficient is $4$. In $x$, the coefficient is $1$ (implied).

Constants

Constant: A term that contains only a number with no variables.
Example: In $3x^2 + 5x + 2$, the constant term is $2$.

Like Terms

Like Terms: Terms that have the same variable(s) raised to the same power. You can combine like terms by adding or subtracting their coefficients.
Example: $3x$ and $5x$ are like terms (both have $x$ to the first power). You can combine them: $3x + 5x = 8x$.
However, $3x$ and $3x^2$ are NOT like terms (different powers).

Exponents (Powers)

Exponent: A small number placed above and to the right of a variable that shows how many times the variable is multiplied by itself.
Example: In $x^3$, the exponent is $3$, which means $x \times x \times x$. In $2x^2$, the coefficient is $2$ and the exponent is $2$.

Order of Operations (PEMDAS/BODMAS)

When working with algebraic expressions, follow this order:

  1. Parentheses/Brackets
  2. Exponents/Orders
  3. Multiplication and Division (left to right)
  4. Addition and Subtraction (left to right)

2. Breaking Down Algebraic Expressions

Primary Example: $\dfrac{5x}{2}$

Expression: $\dfrac{5x}{2}$

Let's break this expression into its parts step by step.

Step 1: Identify the Overall Structure

$$\dfrac{5x}{2}$$ This is a fraction (or rational expression) where:
  • Numerator (top): $5x$
  • Denominator (bottom): $2$

Step 2: Analyze the Numerator

Numerator: $5x$
  • Coefficient: $5$
  • Variable: $x$
  • Meaning: $5$ times $x$

Step 3: Analyze the Denominator

Denominator: $2$
  • This is a constant (a fixed number with no variables)
  • It tells us we're dividing by $2$

Step 4: Interpretation

The expression $\dfrac{5x}{2}$ means:

Take $5$ groups of $x$, then divide that total by $2$. In other words, we split $5x$ into two equal parts, each worth $\dfrac{5x}{2}$.

Step 5: Alternative Forms

An important insight: when we have a fraction with a variable in the numerator and a constant in the denominator, we can rearrange it:

$$\dfrac{5x}{2} = \dfrac{5}{2} \cdot x = 2.5x$$ This shows that $\dfrac{5x}{2}$ is the same as multiplying $x$ by the decimal $2.5$. So if $x = 4$, then:
  • $\dfrac{5(4)}{2} = \dfrac{20}{2} = 10$
  • $2.5 \times 4 = 10$ ✓

Step 6: Component Summary Table

Component What It Is Value/Description
$5$ Coefficient (numerator) The multiplier of $x$
$x$ Variable (numerator) Unknown value in the numerator
$2$ Denominator Constant that we divide by
Division bar Fraction operator Divides numerator by denominator

Additional Examples in Increasing Difficulty

Example 2 (Easy): $3a + 2$

$$3a + 2$$ Breakdown:
  • Number of terms: 2
  • First term: $3a$ → Coefficient: $3$, Variable: $a$
  • Second term: $2$ → Constant (no variables)
  • Operation: $3$ times $a$, then add $2$
  • Example: if $a = 4$, then $3(4) + 2 = 12 + 2 = 14$

Example 3 (Easy): $\dfrac{x}{4}$

$$\dfrac{x}{4}$$ Breakdown:
  • Type: Fraction
  • Numerator: $x$ (coefficient is $1$, implied)
  • Denominator: $4$ (constant)
  • Meaning: Divide $x$ into $4$ equal parts (one quarter of $x$)
  • Alternative forms: $\dfrac{x}{4} = \dfrac{1}{4}x = 0.25x$

Example 4 (Easy): $2x^2$

$$2x^2$$ Breakdown:
  • Coefficient: $2$
  • Variable: $x$
  • Exponent: $2$
  • Meaning: $2$ times ($x$ squared), which equals $2 \times x \times x$
  • Expanded form: $2x^2 = 2 \times x \times x$ (the exponent tells us to multiply $x$ by itself)
  • Example: if $x = 3$, then $2(3)^2 = 2 \times 3 \times 3 = 2 \times 9 = 18$

Example 5 (Medium): $\dfrac{3x + 2}{5}$

$$\dfrac{3x + 2}{5}$$ Breakdown:
  • Type: Fraction with multiple terms in numerator
  • Numerator: $3x + 2$ (this is an expression with 2 terms)
    • Term 1: $3x$ → coefficient $3$, variable $x$
    • Term 2: $2$ → constant
  • Denominator: $5$ (constant)
  • Meaning: Take ($3x$ plus $2$), then divide the entire result by $5$
  • Alternative form: $\dfrac{3x + 2}{5} = \dfrac{3x}{5} + \dfrac{2}{5} = 0.6x + 0.4$
  • Example: if $x = 10$, then $\dfrac{3(10) + 2}{5} = \dfrac{32}{5} = 6.4$, and $0.6(10) + 0.4 = 6 + 0.4 = 6.4$ ✓

Example 6 (Medium): $4(2x + 1)$

$$4(2x + 1)$$ Breakdown:
  • The coefficient $4$ multiplies everything inside the bracket
  • Inside the bracket: $2x + 1$ (expression with 2 terms)
  • Expanded form: $4(2x + 1) = 4 \times 2x + 4 \times 1 = 8x + 4$ (distribute the $4$ to both terms)
  • Terms after expansion: $8x$ (coefficient $8$) and $4$ (constant)
  • Example: if $x = 2$, then $4(2(2) + 1) = 4(4 + 1) = 4(5) = 20$, and $8(2) + 4 = 16 + 4 = 20$ ✓

Example 7 (Medium): $\dfrac{6a}{2b}$

$$\dfrac{6a}{2b}$$ Breakdown:
  • Type: Fraction with variables in both numerator and denominator
  • Numerator: $6a$ → Coefficient: $6$, Variable: $a$
  • Denominator: $2b$ → Coefficient: $2$, Variable: $b$
  • Meaning: Divide ($6$ times $a$) by ($2$ times $b$)
  • Simplified form: $\dfrac{6a}{2b} = \dfrac{3a}{b}$ (we can divide the coefficients: $\dfrac{6}{2} = 3$)
  • Example: if $a = 4$ and $b = 2$, then $\dfrac{6(4)}{2(2)} = \dfrac{24}{4} = 6$, and $\dfrac{3(4)}{2} = \dfrac{12}{2} = 6$ ✓

Example 8 (Hard): $\dfrac{5x + 3y}{2 - z}$

$$\dfrac{5x + 3y}{2 - z}$$ Breakdown:
  • Type: Complex fraction with multiple variables
  • Numerator: $5x + 3y$ (expression with 2 terms)
    • Term 1: $5x$ → coefficient $5$, variable $x$
    • Term 2: $3y$ → coefficient $3$, variable $y$
  • Denominator: $2 - z$ (expression with 2 terms)
    • Term 1: $2$ → constant
    • Term 2: $-z$ → coefficient $-1$, variable $z$
  • Meaning: Divide the sum ($5x + 3y$) by the difference ($2 - z$)
  • Note: This expression cannot be simplified to a single decimal form because it has multiple variables with different values affecting the result
  • Example: if $x = 2$, $y = 1$, and $z = 0$, then $\dfrac{5(2) + 3(1)}{2 - 0} = \dfrac{10 + 3}{2} = \dfrac{13}{2} = 6.5$

Example 9 (Hard): $2(3x^2 + 4x - 5)$

$$2(3x^2 + 4x - 5)$$ Breakdown:
  • The coefficient $2$ multiplies every term inside the bracket
  • Inside the bracket: expression with 3 terms
    • Term 1: $3x^2$ → coefficient $3$, variable $x$, exponent $2$
    • Term 2: $4x$ → coefficient $4$, variable $x$, exponent $1$
    • Term 3: $-5$ → constant
  • Expanded form: $2(3x^2 + 4x - 5) = 6x^2 + 8x - 10$ (distribute the $2$ to each term)
  • Example: if $x = 1$, then $2(3(1)^2 + 4(1) - 5) = 2(3 + 4 - 5) = 2(2) = 4$, and $6(1)^2 + 8(1) - 10 = 6 + 8 - 10 = 4$ ✓

Example 10 (Hard): $\dfrac{2(x + 3)}{y - 1}$

$$\dfrac{2(x + 3)}{y - 1}$$ Breakdown:
  • Type: Complex fraction
  • Numerator: $2(x + 3)$ (coefficient multiplies a bracket)
    • Coefficient outside: $2$
    • Inside bracket: $x + 3$ (two terms: variable $x$ and constant $3$)
    • Expanded form: $2x + 6$
  • Denominator: $y - 1$ (two terms: variable $y$ with coefficient $1$, and constant $-1$)
  • Meaning: Divide ($2$ times the sum of $x$ and $3$) by (the difference of $y$ and $1$)
  • Expanded alternative form: $\dfrac{2(x + 3)}{y - 1} = \dfrac{2x + 6}{y - 1}$ (distribute the $2$ to each term inside the bracket)
  • Example: if $x = 5$ and $y = 3$, then $\dfrac{2(5 + 3)}{3 - 1} = \dfrac{2(8)}{2} = 8$, and $\dfrac{2(5) + 6}{2} = \dfrac{16}{2} = 8$ ✓

3. Practice Questions

Test your understanding of algebraic fundamentals with these practice questions. Questions increase in difficulty from easy to hard.

Easy (Identifying Single Concepts)

Easy
1. In the expression $3x + 5$, what is the coefficient of $x$?
Answer: $3$
The coefficient is the number that multiplies the variable $x$.
Easy
2. In the expression $7y − 4$, what is the constant?
Answer: $−4$
The constant is the number that stands alone without any variables.
Easy
3. How many terms are in the expression $2x + 3y − 8$?
Answer: 3 terms
The three terms are: $2x$, $3y$, and $−8$. Terms are separated by $+$ or $−$ signs.
Easy
4. What is the variable in $9a + 4$?
Answer: $a$
The variable is the letter that represents an unknown number.
Easy
5. In the term $−5x$, what is the coefficient?
Answer: $−5$
The coefficient includes the sign, so it's negative 5.

Medium (Identifying Components in Expressions)

Medium
6. In $4x^2 + 2x − 7$, identify: (a) the coefficient of $x^2$, (b) the coefficient of $x$, and (c) the constant.
Answer:
(a) Coefficient of $x^2$: $4$
(b) Coefficient of $x$: $2$
(c) Constant: $−7$
Medium
7. Are $3x^2$ and $5x$ like terms? Explain why or why not.
Answer: No, they are not like terms.
$3x^2$ has $x$ to the power of $2$, while $5x$ has $x$ to the power of $1$. Like terms must have the same variable(s) with the same exponents.
Medium
8. Simplify by combining like terms: $5x + 3y + 2x − y$
Answer: $7x + 2y$
Combine $x$ terms: $5x + 2x = 7x$
Combine $y$ terms: $3y − y = 2y$
Result: $7x + 2y$
Medium
9. In the expression $6ab − 2a + 4b$, how many terms are there and what are they?
Answer: 3 terms: $6ab$, $−2a$, and $4b$
Each term is separated by a $+$ or $−$ sign. The term $6ab$ has a coefficient of $6$ with both variables $a$ and $b$.
Medium
10. What is the coefficient of $xy$ in the term $−7xy$?
Answer: $−7$
The coefficient is the number multiplying all the variables together. Here it's $−7$ times $xy$.

Hard (Breaking Down Complex Expressions)

Hard
11. Break down $\dfrac{5x}{2}$ into its component parts. Identify: (a) the numerator, (b) the denominator, (c) the coefficient in the numerator, (d) the variable in the numerator.
Answer:
(a) Numerator: $5x$
(b) Denominator: $2$
(c) Coefficient in the numerator: $5$
(d) Variable in the numerator: $x$
Hard
12. In the expression $3(2x + 4y − 5)$, expand it and then identify all coefficients and constants in the expanded form.
Answer: Expanded: $6x + 12y − 15$
Coefficients: $6$ (for $x$), $12$ (for $y$)
Constant: $−15$
We multiply each term inside the bracket by $3$: $3(2x) = 6x$, $3(4y) = 12y$, $3(−5) = −15$
Hard
13. The expression $\dfrac{3x − 2}{5}$ represents: (a) What is being divided by what? (b) Identify the numerator and denominator. (c) What are the terms in the numerator?
Answer:
(a) $(3x − 2)$ is being divided by $5$
(b) Numerator: $3x − 2$; Denominator: $5$
(c) The terms in the numerator are: $3x$ (coefficient $3$, variable $x$) and $−2$ (constant)
Hard
14. Simplify: $2(3x + 4) − 5x$. Show all steps and identify the coefficient and constant in your final answer.
Answer:
Step 1: Expand $2(3x + 4) = 6x + 8$
Step 2: So the expression is: $6x + 8 − 5x$
Step 3: Combine like terms: $6x − 5x = x$
Final: $x + 8$
Coefficient of $x$: $1$ (implied)
Constant: $8$
Hard
15. In the expression $4x^2y + 3xy^2 − 2xy + 5$, identify: (a) all the terms, (b) all the coefficients, (c) the constant, and (d) which terms are NOT like terms and why.
Answer:
(a) Terms: $4x^2y$, $3xy^2$, $−2xy$, and $5$
(b) Coefficients: $4$, $3$, $−2$
(c) Constant: $5$
(d) None of the first three terms are like terms because:
- $4x^2y$ has $x^2$ and $y^1$
- $3xy^2$ has $x^1$ and $y^2$
- $−2xy$ has $x^1$ and $y^1$
All have different variable combinations or powers.
Hard
16. Break down and analyze: $\dfrac{2(x + 3)}{y − 1}$. Identify: (a) the numerator, (b) the denominator, (c) all variables, (d) all constants, and (e) the coefficient outside the bracket in the numerator.
Answer:
(a) Numerator: $2(x + 3)$
(b) Denominator: $(y − 1)$
(c) Variables: $x$ and $y$
(d) Constants: $3$ and $−1$
(e) Coefficient outside the bracket in the numerator: $2$
